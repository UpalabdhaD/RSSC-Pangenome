# Response to Reviewer Comment 5

**Reviewer comment:** The authors find that genes in integration spots display elevated Ka/Ks relative to housekeeping functions. They rightly urge caution when interpreting omega > 1 for recently acquired genes due to low synonymous divergence. For the “absent from major lineages” category, 84% of genes showed Ka/Ks > 1. While this supports “arms-race” evolution, the risk of technical artifacts (alignment errors in highly divergent accessory genes) remains high. Including a sensitivity analysis or using a different metric (like pN/pS) for the most variable compartments could strengthen this claim.

**Response:** We thank the reviewer for highlighting this important limitation. We agree that a Ka/Ks value greater than one is not, by itself, sufficient evidence for arms-race evolution in recently acquired or highly divergent genes. In particular, low synonymous divergence, saturation, short alignments, and alignment errors can all inflate or destabilize the ratio. We have therefore treated the original result as evidence consistent with, rather than proof of, recurrent diversifying selection.

The reported 84% corresponds to 90 of 106 genes in the “absent from major lineages” category. We performed a sensitivity analysis using synonymous-divergence, alignment-length, sequence-completeness, gap, ambiguity, and internal-stop-codon filters. The result was not explained by the low-Ks filter: requiring Ks >= 0.05 retained 90/106 genes (84.9%), identical in numerator and denominator to the unfiltered result. Requiring an alignment length of at least 300 nt retained 71/83 genes (85.5%). The equal-length, gap, ambiguity, and internal-stop filters each retained 90/106 genes (84.9%). Applying the combined Ks and alignment-quality criteria retained 71/83 genes (85.5%). Thus, the high fraction is robust to the principal quality filters, although the denominator becomes smaller under the combined filter.

We also examined a restricted set with Ks <= 2 to assess the effect of very high synonymous divergence; 66 of 70 genes exceeded one (94.3%). We do not interpret this higher fraction as stronger evidence, because the additional filtering changes the estimand and further reduces the sample size. Conversely, a strict minimum of four sequences leaves only three genes, of which one exceeds one (33.3%). This small-denominator result reinforces the need to report the number of genes and the uncertainty around category-level percentages rather than relying on the percentage alone.

As an independent method check, we compared the completed PAML codeml M0 estimates with the previously used KaKs_Calculator NG estimates. Among 6,271 matched gene families, PAML gave a median omega of 0.0955 compared with 0.2437 for the previous Ka/Ks estimates. PAML classified 121/6,271 (1.9%) as omega > 1, whereas the previous estimates classified 1,374/6,271 (21.9%) as Ka/Ks > 1. The methods had a Spearman correlation of 0.481, but agreement on the binary >1 classification was limited (Cohen’s kappa = 0.084); most disagreements were genes called positive by the previous ratio but purifying by PAML (1,287 genes), rather than the reverse (34 genes). This comparison supports our decision not to interpret the earlier Ka/Ks > 1 calls as definitive evidence of positive selection. The PAML analysis is currently complete for 9,187 of 9,624 eligible families (95.5%); therefore, we present it as a complementary validation rather than a complete replacement analysis.

We agree that a formally modeled pN/pS analysis would be a useful additional test. We have not presented the existing substitution-count ratio as an independent pN/pS estimate, because it does not model synonymous and nonsynonymous opportunities in the same way as a full pN/pS analysis. Accordingly, we have retained the sensitivity analysis and the PAML comparison as safeguards, and we have softened the interpretation of this category: the elevated ratio is consistent with diversifying selection in lineage-restricted genes, but technical artifacts and the biology of recent acquisition remain plausible alternative or contributing explanations. We will avoid wording that treats the result alone as establishing an arms-race mechanism.
